The 5 Phases

1. Empathize
The Empathize phase is the cornerstone of Design Thinking, requiring a deep understanding of user needs and perspectives. As the first stage of the Design Thinking process, it is the cornerstone of this human-centered process. It's all about deeply understanding the problem, from the perspective of the people who the solution will impact or who the design is for—their experiences, emotions, needs, and challenges. This phase is about more than just gathering data; it's about connecting with users on a human level to uncover their unspoken needs and pain points.

Warning Remember Design Thinking at its core is ALL about being Human-Centered. It's about viewing the problem from the position of those affected by it.

2. Define
The Define phase is where you synthesize all of your insights to identify the core problem. This involves analyzing the data gathered during the Empathize phase to pinpoint the fundamental issue(s). This typically involves a process called Affinity Mapping, where common data points are grouped by category or relatedness, which helps cluster data to identify patterns, and begin developing a problem statement that is framed from the user's perspective, which you can then convert into a clearly defined Problem Statement, the output of the Define Phase.
3. Ideate
In the Ideate phase, the goal is to generate a wide range of possible solutions to the now, clearly defined problem. This phase encourages collaborative, creative brainstorming to generate ideas. It's about quantity over quality initially, aiming to create an ample and diverse set of ideas to be refined later. No idea is a bad idea! No idea is to far out there!
4. Prototype
The Prototype phase is where ideas begin to take tangible form. This stage focuses on creating low-cost, scaled-down models or mockups of potential solutions. Prototypes can be sketches, digital wireframes, storyboards, role-play exercises, or simple physical models—anything that helps make an abstract idea concrete and testable. The goal is not perfection but learning; prototypes are intentionally quick and low-fidelity so that multiple variations can be developed and compared rapidly. By externalizing ideas, teams can explore feasibility, reveal hidden challenges, and identify opportunities for improvement before committing significant time and resources.
5. Test
The Test phase is the critical checkpoint where prototypes are put in front of real users for feedback. This stage allows designers to validate whether the solution addresses the defined problem, resonates with user needs, and functions in practical contexts. Testing is iterative and often uncovers unexpected insights, highlighting both strengths and flaws of the proposed design. Importantly, testing is not the end of the process but a means to refine and improve. Feedback gathered here informs adjustments to prototypes or even reframing the problem if necessary, ensuring the final solution is both effective and user-centered.
ITERATE
Iteration and continuous feedback are the lifeblood of Design Thinking. Rather than a linear march toward a single solution, the process emphasizes cycles of learning, testing, and refining. Insights from each stage feed forward and backward, allowing teams to pivot, adapt, and improve based on real-world input. This cyclical nature not only reduces the risk of failure but also fosters innovation by encouraging experimentation and resilience. By embracing iteration, teams cultivate solutions that evolve alongside user needs, ensuring that outcomes are both relevant and impactful.
